# Oliver Live Trial Script V1

Use this for a 20-30 minute moderated trial with a PM, product lead, launch owner, or cross-functional operator.

## Goal

Decide whether Oliver feels like a real execution aid on messy threads, not just a cleaner summary generator.

## Setup

- Prepare 2 real threads from the participant's world.
- Prefer one thread that is messy but active.
- Prefer one thread that is incomplete, conflicting, or low-signal.
- Do not pre-clean the threads for Oliver.
- Ask the participant to think aloud.

## Script

### 1. Framing (2 minutes)

Say:

- "We are testing whether this can turn a messy execution thread into something you would actually use to drive follow-through."
- "We care as much about wrong structure and false confidence as we do about helpful summaries."

### 2. First thread: active launch or rollout (8-10 minutes)

Ask the participant to paste a real thread.

Prompt:

- "Pretend you just inherited this thread and need to know whether launch is actually on track."

Have them review:

- launch goal
- owners
- dates or launch window
- dependencies
- blockers
- decisions
- readiness brief
- drafted follow-ups

Ask:

- "What is the first thing here you would trust?"
- "What is the first thing you would double-check manually?"
- "Would you use this to run the next status meeting?"
- "Would you approve or send any of the follow-up drafts with light edits?"

### 3. Second thread: incomplete or conflicting (8-10 minutes)

Prompt:

- "Now give it a thread where the signal is weak or the status is conflicting."

Ask:

- "Did Oliver preserve uncertainty or did it invent too much structure?"
- "Did it correctly avoid sounding confident?"
- "Did the readiness judgment feel earned?"
- "If this were in production, would this output increase or decrease your trust?"

### 4. Comparative question (3-5 minutes)

Ask:

- "How different is this from a good summary tool?"
- "What would have to improve before you would rely on this for real launch follow-through?"
- "Would you hand it a second thread after seeing these two?"

## Moderator Notes

- Capture any place where the participant says "I would still have to read the thread myself."
- Capture any place where they say "this makes me more confident" or "this would save me time."
- Mark whether trust breaks because of:
  - wrong owner
  - wrong date
  - wrong blocker
  - fake certainty
  - low-quality follow-up draft
  - missing operationally important issue

## Success Signals

- Participant says the readiness brief changed what they would do next.
- Participant would use at least part of the plan in a real follow-up.
- Participant would approve at least one drafted follow-up with light edits.
- Participant would forward or paste another thread voluntarily.

## Failure Signals

- Participant treats it as a summary they still have to fully re-parse.
- Participant spots hallucinated structure quickly.
- Participant says the readiness brief sounds generic or optimistic.
- Participant would not trust the follow-up drafts enough to send.
